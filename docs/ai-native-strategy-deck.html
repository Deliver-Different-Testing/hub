DFRNT strategy deck

What AI-native could mean for DFRNT and Urgent

A strategic discussion deck to help the team understand the future-state vision, the path to get there, and the three real options in front of us.

Prepared from the current AI-native strategy pack, white paper, and latest strategic framing update.

01
Why now

This is not a “how do we use more AI?” conversation

The wrong frame
  • Add copilots to current workflows
  • Automate a few admin tasks
  • Project today’s org chart and systems into the future
  • Optimise the current courier model at the edges
The right frame
  • Start with a compelling 5–7 year vision
  • Ask what an AI-native operating model actually looks like
  • Backcast the systems, workflows and capabilities needed
  • Decide whether we are building a better old model — or a new one
02

03
Future-state vision

What are we backcasting from?

If we designed DFRNT and Urgent from scratch today to be AI-native 5–7 years from now, what would exist, what would be automated, what would humans still own, and what would make the business structurally better than a traditional courier operator?

Continuously learning operating system

Operational events are captured once and reused everywhere. The platform gets smarter from real execution, not reports written after the fact.

Exception-led human work

Agents, rules and workflows handle repetitive coordination. Humans focus on exceptions, judgement, service design and commercial decisions.

Better scaling economics

Quality, speed and margin improve without management overhead scaling at the same rate.

04
What AI-native means

AI-native is not “chatbot everywhere”

Visible

The data exists and can be seen.

→

Assisted

AI drafts, suggests, classifies and summarises.

→

Automated

Low-risk work completes end-to-end through rules and agents.

Core design idea

  • Every stage has a clean system of record
  • Key events are emitted once and reused everywhere
  • Apps are organised around operating outcomes, not historical team boundaries

End-state discipline

  • Humans stay on judgement, exceptions and relationships
  • Machines take coordination, classification and repetitive decision support
  • The system reprioritises around cost, SLA risk and customer value
05
Operating model

The four operating stages

Stage 01

Sales

Win the customer, price the work, configure the service, and hand off cleanly into live operations.

Stage 02

Operations

Turn demand into planned, controlled, profitable execution through orchestration and exception control.

Stage 03

Accounts

Bill accurately, settle correctly, reconcile fast, and surface margin truth.

Stage 04

Delivery

Execute the work in the field, capture proof, exceptions and customer visibility.

Lead → Quote → Onboard → Book → Plan → Dispatch → Deliver → Invoice → Settle → Report → Improve

07
Strategic options

There are three credible paths

Option 1

Stay AI-enabled

  • Add selective AI features to the current model
  • Lowest near-term disruption
  • Highest risk of becoming a cleaner version of the old model
Option 2

Dual-track: defend + wedge

  • Usually sounds balanced
  • Works only if “defend the base” does not consume all oxygen
  • High risk of delaying the real pivot
Option 3

Full AI-native pivot

  • Make the pivot the primary build priority
  • Use Urgent as the proof case
  • Best fit if the aim is structural change, not incremental optimisation
08
Trade-offs

What each option gives up

Option 1 risk

We end up with better tooling, but not a structurally better business.

Option 2 risk

Legacy work absorbs the team and the wedge never becomes the strategy.

Option 3 risk

Requires discipline, proof milestones, and the willingness to stop saying yes to work that drags us backwards.

Biggest external risk

Prospects keep asking DFRNT to preserve manual process steps that an AI-native strategy is supposed to eliminate.

Biggest internal risk

We confuse AI novelty with operating-model change and call that progress too early.

09
Execution discipline

What has to be true if we want to win

  • Urgent is treated as the proof case, not just another tenant
  • The roadmap is ranked against the target operating model
  • Build capacity for the AI-native wedge is protected
  • New work is judged on whether it moves us toward the future-state vision
  • We measure proof in real operating metrics
  • We keep the system-of-record and event model clean
  • We do not let tenant-by-tenant requests define the product architecture
  • We build a testimony engine from the transformation story

Recommendation

The strongest direction is a more decisive AI-native pivot — but grounded in proof milestones, real operating metrics, and roadmap discipline.
